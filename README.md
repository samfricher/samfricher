### Currently building · [Magnesium](https://github.com/samfricher/magnesium)

AI can explain almost anything to anyone, with unlimited patience, so learning should have become easy. It has not. In a field experiment with about a thousand high-school students, the students given an unguarded chatbot for practice solved more practice problems and then scored 17 percent lower on the exam than the students given nothing (Bastani et al., PNAS, 2025). The same pattern appears at work. In a survey of 319 knowledge workers, the more a worker trusted the AI on a task, the less critical thinking the worker reported doing on that task (Lee et al., CHI, 2025). That is knowledge debt: the task gets done, the skill never forms, and the debt grows as AI can do more of the work.

The explanations are good. The problem is what happens around them. Reading a good explanation feels like learning, and producing the answer yourself, with the explanation closed, is what builds the skill. A chatbot that answers every question removes the need to ever produce anything.

Magnesium is my attempt at the fix. It is a local, harness-agnostic learning platform. Harness-agnostic means it works with whichever coding agent you already use, Claude Code, Codex or OpenCode, and that is possible because everything Magnesium knows about you lives in files in your folder rather than inside any one tool. Set it up in any folder with one command, `magnesium init`. The tutor explains briefly, then asks you to reproduce what it explained. When you fail, it finds the step that broke, repairs that step, and asks again after a gap. Every answer goes into a learner record in that folder, so later practice goes to what keeps failing, and the record travels with the folder to whichever agent you open next. The material can be lecture notes, a textbook, a paper or the codebase you are working in.
